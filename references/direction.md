# Direction Discovery

## Establish The Baseline

- Render the current product before suggesting a direction.
- Identify its primary job, audience, frequent actions, required states, and visual commitments.
- Separate confirmed facts, user preferences, and assumptions.

## Resolve Taste

Ask only unresolved questions that could change the design. Prefer concrete contrasts: dense or spacious, quiet or expressive, editorial or operational, sharp or soft. Ask for references when words are ambiguous.

In Hands-off mode, infer answers and mark material assumptions so the user can replace them later.

## Research Safely

Prefer primary pages and working products. Search by relevant property rather than copying one product. Record the URL and lesson. Never claim access you lacked; ask the user for evidence when blocked.

Extract principles, not complete designs, brand identities, signature illustrations, or proprietary assets.

Keep private project material local unless the user gives provider-specific consent. Redact screenshots and prompts before approved external transfer.

## Generate Directions

Make each direction a coherent system, not a color variation. Define:

- a short name and design thesis;
- hierarchy, type, palette, spacing, geometry, imagery, and motion;
- how primary tasks and important states appear;
- benefits, risks, and what the direction deliberately avoids.

Guided mode may present three directions. Full Studio prototypes three to five distinct directions. Use the smallest rendered artifact that exposes the real tradeoff; use the same content and viewport.

Do not average incompatible directions. Record compatible combined traits as one explicit system.

## Direction Checkpoint

Ask the user to select, reject, or modify a direction from comparable evidence. Record the decision and anti-goals. Do not begin broad implementation without approval unless Hands-off applies.
